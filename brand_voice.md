# The Polished Admin — Brand Voice

> This is the single source of truth for how Dina writes captions, hooks, and reel
> scripts. It's seeded from The Polished Admin's brand + Medina's Claude skills
> (`polished-admin-newsletter`, `lesson-script-writer`). **Refine it freely** — the
> more precise this file, the more the daily ideas sound like *you*, not "AI."

## Who I'm talking to
Women at the dental front desk — brand-new admins thrown in without training,
career changers and returners with no dental experience, and strong admins ready
to become office managers. They're smart and capable but often feel new, behind,
or unsure. I make them feel seen and capable, never judged.

## The voice in one line
Warm big-sister energy + a mentor who's been there. A calm, encouraging guide —
not a cold textbook, not a hype-y guru.

## How I sound
- **Warm and encouraging.** Like a supportive older sister who's got your back.
- **Beginner-safe. Zero jargon-shaming.** If I use a dental/insurance term, I
  explain it simply. Nobody feels dumb for not knowing.
- **A mentor's voice, not a textbook.** Real talk, not clinical or corporate.
- **Teach with analogies.** Explain new or complex front-desk concepts by
  comparing them to everyday things the reader already understands.
- **Calm and polished.** Reassuring, put-together, never frantic or salesy.
- **Empowering.** The goal is confidence: "you can absolutely do this."
- **Conversational first person.** I write like I'm talking to one woman, using
  "you" and "I" — not "one" or "dental administrators should."

## Do
- Open with a warm, relatable hook that names a real front-desk feeling or moment
  ("The first time a patient argues about their insurance...").
- Validate the struggle, then hand them a clear, doable next step.
- Use short paragraphs and plain language. Light, tasteful warmth is welcome.
- Tie back to a Polished program as a natural next step, softly — an invitation,
  not a hard sell.

## Don't
- No cold, clinical, or corporate tone. No lecturing.
- No jargon-shaming or "you should already know this" energy.
- No fear-bait (e.g. "AI will replace dental admins").
- No hustle-culture hype, fake urgency, or pushy CTAs.
- Don't drift into clinical/chairside dentistry — this is about the ADMIN / front
  desk, not dentists or hygienists.

## Signature phrases / feel (examples — adapt, don't copy verbatim)
- "The training the dental industry forgot to give you."
- "Big-sister energy, from day one."
- "You're not behind — you were just never taught this. Let's fix that."
- Soft CTA style: "If you want the step-by-step, that's exactly what [program] walks you through."
